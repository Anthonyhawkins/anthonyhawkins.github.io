Today I'm launching this journal. I'm a senior networking engineer — TCP, gRPC, edge protocols, payments infrastructure — and I'm betting my next career chapter on space.

## Why Space?

Commercialized satellite constellations are the next major internet infrastructure buildout. SpaceX has 6,000+ satellites in orbit. Amazon's Kuiper is launching this year. The Space Development Agency is building a military mesh network in low Earth orbit. All of these systems need people who understand networking — not just RF engineering, not just aerospace — **networking**.

My TCP background maps directly to problems like Licklider Transmission Protocol (LTP), the Delay-Tolerant Networking Bundle Protocol, and contact graph routing. My gRPC experience maps to ground station orchestration. My edge protocol optimization experience maps to satellite handover and constellation traffic engineering.

## The Plan

I'm following a 52-week parallel-track curriculum:

- **🔢 Math track** — Algebra 2 → Trig → Pre-Calc → Calculus I/II/III → Linear Algebra → Probability → Graph Theory
- **💻 C++ track** — learning C++ for systems/protocol programming
- **🛰️ Space networking** — protocols, standards (CCSDS, DTN, DVB-S2), and hands-on projects

Six of the twelve curriculum modules need **no math at all** — they're pure protocol architecture, and my networking background is the prerequisite. So I can start reading CCSDS specs and RFC 9171 (Bundle Protocol v7) tomorrow.
